3. What SquidSpot is, and what it isn’t

SquidSpot is a planning companion for land-based squid fishing (eging) in Australia. It shows map pins at public piers, jetties, rock walls, boat ramps and similar places, a Squid score estimate, forecast wind and swell, predicted tide times, jig suggestions, a catch log kept on your phone, and a shareable spot card.

Pins are “checked public-report” spots. That means they are based on publicly available catch reports and community information, which we have checked against those reports. We do not visit, inspect, survey or verify spots. A pin is not a statement that a place is safe, open, legal to access or legal to fish, or that squid are there.

SquidSpot is not an official source. It is not run, approved or endorsed by the Bureau of Meteorology, any government or fisheries agency, any port or marine authority, or any land owner or manager.

Nothing in SquidSpot is legal, safety, navigation, marine or other professional advice.

5. Forecasts, tides and scores are estimates

Wind and swell are computer-model forecasts from third-party weather providers such as Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), delivered through our forecast server. Tide times are predictions calculated on your phone from tide-gauge constants (TICON-4, CC BY 4.0), and in some places may come from modelled sea levels. Predicted tide times can differ from official tide tables by several minutes or more, especially far from a tide gauge.

The Squid score (also shown as the SquidSpot score), “Squid score tonight” tags, session windows, tide phase, water-clarity estimate and jig suggestions are SquidSpot’s own estimates. They are not official forecasts and not a prediction that you will catch anything.

Do not use SquidSpot for navigation, boating, safety or emergency decisions, to decide whether conditions are safe, or to decide whether fishing in a place or in a particular way is lawful. Always check official sources, including Bureau of Meteorology forecasts and warnings (bom.gov.au), official tide tables, and the fisheries rules for your state or territory.

Catches are never guaranteed.

Many of these are obvious risks.

You decide whether, where, when and how to fish, and you do so at your own risk. You are solely responsible for your own safety and for the safety of anyone with you, including children in your care.

Before and during every trip, check official Bureau of Meteorology forecasts and warnings, local conditions and signs, and follow directions from authorities, rangers, port operators and land managers. Do not fish from rocks or exposed structures in dangerous conditions. Wear suitable safety gear (for example a lifejacket and non-slip footwear on rocks), avoid fishing alone in risky places, tell someone your plans, carry a charged phone and a light at night, and leave if conditions turn.

SquidSpot does not monitor conditions at any spot and cannot warn you of hazards. In an emergency, call 000.
